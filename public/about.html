CoAuth is an agent-native prior-authorization cockpit. It lets a clinician and an AI agent complete a health-insurance prior authorization together on one live page, using the WebMCP standard. Instead of an AI agent scraping the page and guessing where to click, CoAuth exposes the payer form as typed tools that the agent calls directly - filling fields, attaching evidence, scoring denial risk, and detecting clinical conflicts at machine speed.

The human stays firmly in control. The agent cannot fabricate clinician-judgment fields, cannot resolve a critical clinical contraindication, and cannot submit. Submission is architecturally gated behind a clinician signature: the submit tool returns a "blocked" status until a human reviews the work, attests to its accuracy, and signs. This mirrors the legal reality that high-stakes clinical decisions require accountable human oversight.

Why it matters: up to 82% of denied prior authorizations are overturned on appeal. The clinical decision was rarely wrong - the paperwork was. CoAuth fixes the filing so patients get care sooner, while keeping a licensed human accountable for every submission. It was built for the OpenAI WebMCP Challenge as a demonstration of genuine human + agent collaboration on a real, high-stakes workflow.

The project is open source under the MIT license. See the source repository for the full implementation, including the 12 WebMCP tools, the denial-risk engine, and the conflict-detection rules.
